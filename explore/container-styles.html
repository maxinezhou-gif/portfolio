Image container styles

Six treatments of the same two pieces of media, so the choice is made by looking rather than by describing. Everything is the real figure markup and the real stylesheet — only the frame and the media's own edge change between them.

Shadows read very differently against white than against the site's #FAFAFA, and the portfolio is the darker one. Switch the background before deciding.

Playground

Dial the corner and the shadow with the bar above. When it looks right, read the numbers off and I will move them into case-study.css.

A

The card — what ships today

A tinted panel, a hairline and 20px of padding, with the media clipped to 16px inside it. The inset is the whole idea: the media is a specimen on a tray. It is also why nothing in a figure can line up with the text column.

B

Bare — no container at all

The media's edges are the figure's edges. This is what the hero uses now, and why it lines up with "Maxine Zhou" and "About". Honest, but a light UI screenshot on a light page has nothing holding its edge.

C

Niklas — 4px and a soft shadow

Measured off niklas.space/canvas: no panel, border-radius: 4px, and five stacked grey shadows offset very slightly right and down. The small corner is doing as much work as the shadow — it reads as a photograph of a screen, not a UI element.

D

His shadow, your corner

The same ramp at the site's 16px radius. Keeps the shadow idea without importing a second corner language into a stylesheet that already has four.

E

Hairline only

The card's edge without the card — a 1px ring on the media itself, drawn as a shadow so it costs no layout and cannot push the media out of alignment.

F

Ring and lift

A quieter shadow with the hairline kept. On #FAFAFA a grey shadow has very little room to be seen; the ring guarantees the edge and the shadow only has to suggest the lift.
