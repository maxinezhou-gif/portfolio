h2   | Background & Context
p    | Underwriters work in a high-volume, high-stakes environment where speed and accuracy are essential. The application’s workflow centered around two primary pages:
p    | Pipeline View : A table-based workspace for filtering, triaging, and updating submissions
p    | Submission Detail : A deep-dive view containing full submission context, documents, analysis, and activity history
h2   | Pipeline View Page
h2   | Submission Detail Page (deeper dive)
h3   | Start at UX audit
p    | As the sole product designer brought in to shape the next version of this product, I began with a structured UX audit to identify improvement opportunities.
h3   | Understanding Real Usage
p    | Following the UX audit, I analysed the funnel behaviour, heat maps, feature usage and session data to understand how underwriters actually worked.
h3   | Where does decision-making actually happen?
h3   | What information do users need during triage?
h3   | How can the interface better support comparison across submissions?
h3   | Are users navigating efficiently or compensating for missing context?
h3   | What the Data Revealed
p    | 1. Only 46.1% of users entered the submission detail page
p    | 2. The Back button was the most-used interaction on submission detail
p    | 3. Users frequently looped between pipeline and submission detail
p    | 4. Submission detail was used selectively to access specific context, especially summaries and notes
h3   | Why Back button dominance?
p    | The answer: fast comprehension first, depth on demand
p    | The pipeline view functioned as the true decision workspace, while the submission detail page acted as a short-term inspection layer.
p    | Underwriters relied on the pipeline page for orientation and comparison, opening submission detail primarily to verify context before returning to continue their review.
h2   | Design Direction
p    | 1. Rebalanced information hierarchy to surface key signals closer to the pipeline
p    | 2. Cut navigation overhead with pre-filtered views and a 'filters-on-demand' system
p    | 3. Introduced a contextual Details drawer for files, AI insights, activity history, and notes
p    | 4. Implemented an adaptive viewport to support dense data analysis without losing workflow context
h3   | I proposed a design direction that improved the workflow and resolved key UX friction points:
h3   | Reducing Noise, Preserving Control
p    | I designed a ‘filters-on-demand’ system that reduced visual complexity while maintaining flexibility. Frequently used filters were prioritised, and reusable filter presets allowed underwriters to quickly re-establish context across sessions.
p    | I proposed consolidating key submission information into a contextual drawer accessible from the pipeline page.
h2   | Adaptive Viewport for High-Density Data
p    | To accommodate high-density data, I designed an adaptive viewport that supports uninterrupted data analysis while preserving quick access to workflow context and collaboration tools.
h2   | Impact
p    | With over 224 hours of monthly usage, even small improvements could scale quickly. Reducing just one minute per session could save over 45 hours per month across underwriting teams.
p    | Due to roadmap priorities, this work was positioned as a future optimisation, establishing a clear, data-backed direction for improving underwriting efficiency at scale.

h1   | Enterprise Design System Migration Challenge
h2   | Led design system migration across a 16-product insurance ecosystem, cutting custom components by 94% and establishing governance across three design agencies.
span | Design System
span | Design Ops
span | Strategy
span | InsurTech
h2   | Overview
h3   | Our Design Team
p    | Design System Designer (me)
p    | + Design Manager
h3   | Timeline
p    | March - December 2025
p    | (10 months)
h3   | My Core Deliverables
li   | Four products migrated with three in active progress
li   | Design library fully built, aligned with storybook
li   | Documentation and governance frameworks established
li   | Design team culture transformed from siloed to collaborative
li   | Client set up to scale the system independently after the engagement ended
h2   | The Product
p    | An internal underwriting application for insurance teams to review and triage submissions, manage workflows, and make faster and more confident decisions
h2   | Impacts
h4   | 49
h3   | with full tokenization and documentation
h4   | 35% → 2%
h3   | reduction in custom components (core product pilot)
h4   | 10
h3   | designers across three agencies aligned and collaborative
h3   | Migrated or in-progress within engagement period
h3   | The Challenge
p    | The client operated 16+ products built by multiple contractors with significant operational challenges:
li   | 83% of design work required custom components due to system limitations of the existing design system
li   | Three separate design agencies worked in silos with inconsistent practices
li   | Designers skipped collaboration meetings and worked independently
li   | Fragmented user experiences across the product portfolio
li   | No clear governance on when/how to extend the design system
h3   | What my role brought
li   | Build and organise the design library and documentation
li   | Provide design system services and support to designers across agencies
li   | Establish governance processes for consistent adoption
li   | Lead pilot migration to demonstrate value and approach
li   | Transform team culture from siloed to collaborative
p    | After
p    | Before
h3   | My Approach
h6   | Phase 1
h2   | Foundation Building
p    | 1. Built the Design Library covering full product ecosystem
p    | 2. Tokenised Design Foundations + Component Documentation
p    | 3. Established the cleaned-up version of Storybook as the single source of truth
p    | 4. Provided designer support infrastructure across all three agencies
h6   | Phase 2
h2   | Governance & Quality Assurance
p    | 1. Conducted UX audits + regular design checkin across agencies to identify drift
p    | 2. Documented Exception Framework (on the right) for when deviations make sense, balancing system integrity with design flexibility
p    | 3. Created 3-4 week component prioritisation pipeline
p    | 4. Hosted Pattern Workshops across agencies to identify reusable patterns
h2   | The Challenge I faced at phase 2
p    | What happened
p    | While reviewing some mockups, I noticed many components were heavily customised — spacing, padding, and text weights differed from the DS.
p    | The tension
p    | Product designers wanted to preserve user familiarity.
p    | The DS manager questioned the purpose of migration if visuals remain unchanged.
p    | What I did to solve the underlying conflict
p    | 1. Clarified intent: Migration = technical adoption + gradual alignment, not forced visual conformity.
p    | 2. Defined path forward:
p    | Phase 1 – DS components with overrides;
p    | Phase 2 – reassess after research and user acceptance testing.
p    | 3. Process improvement: Proposed an Exceptional Framework to document justified divergences from the DS.
h3   | Phase 3
h2   | Strategic Migration
p    | We ran a low-risk pilot on a simple product to validate the process and build team confidence. Then, we migrated the essential product to showcase impact.
p    | During migration, we fixed some UX issues, applied semantic tokens, and improved predictability with a unified visual language.
p    | (Result: 35% → 2% custom components)
h3   | Phase 4
h2   | Scaling & Enablement
p    | 1. Transformed siloed agencies into collaborative community
p    | 2. Completed four product migrations, three in progress (planned completion by end of year)
p    | 3. Client equipped to continue independently with established processes
h3   | Impact for the business
li   | Drastic reduction in design/handoff time as DS coverage increased
li   | Migration approach proven through pilot that other teams can replicate
li   | Team now owns and evolves the design system together
h3   | Reflection and learnings
li   | I've learned that DS migration is as much about people and process as components, building trust among design teams by providing supports proactively is the key.
li   | I also understood the importance of change management: balancing consistency with designers' needs.
li   | Stakeholder communication and systems thinking are essential!!
li   | Finally, I've learnt to choose the battle. We decided to pause the migration on one product which only has less than 10 active users as we feel designers of this product is reluctant do the migration in our suggested way, and it’s time consuming to repeat and try to onboard them at every design check-in meeting.

h1   | AI-Augmented Contract documents Analysis Platform
h2   | Designed trust into AI-powered contract analysis for high-pressure insurance workflows
span | AI / ML
span | B2B SaaS
span | Workflow
span | InsurTech
h2   | Overview
h2   | My Role
p    | Product Designer (Sole Designer)
h2   | Key Outcomes
li   | Streamlined contract review during critical renewal periods
li   | Designed Continuous AI Training in workflow that felt natural to users
li   | Built user trust through transparent AI verification design
h2   | Additional Complexity
li   | Remote collaboration with a dev team in France
li   | Tech constraints: LLM latency and inability to build a specific layout
li   | Ecosystem consistency: aligned with two products and fed insights into a core product
h2   | The Product
p    | An internal underwriting application for insurance teams to review and triage submissions, manage workflows, and make faster and more confident decisions
h2   | The Problem
h3   | Fragmented, High-Stakes Workflows
p    | During busy renewal periods, underwriters analyse hundreds of reinsurance and insurance contracts under tight deadlines. Their workflow is chaotic—constantly switching between:
li   | Contract PDFs
li   | Email chains
li   | Notes documents
li   | Slack conversations
li   | Dropbox files
h3   | Need a solution that could
li   | Make contract review instantaneous, not time-consuming
li   | Simplify checking against complex underwriting rules
li   | Reduce compliance risk and financial exposure
h3   | How might we accelerate contract analysis without compromising thoroughness, accuracy, or user confidence in AI-generated insights?
h2   | Research and Discovery
p    | I conducted stakeholder interviews with underwriters and product managers to understand their workflows, pain points, and needs during high-pressure renewal periods.
h3   | Key Insights that shaped design
li   | Navigation must be instantaneous and mirror document’s natural flow
li   | Users question AI accuracy, design should communicate transparency and verifiable sources
li   | AI assistance should feel like productive work, not extra overhead
h2   | Design Evolution
p    | I went through three distinct layout approaches, testing each with PMs and users to land on the optimal information hierarchy that also fit within the dev team's technical constraints.
h3   | Early Prototype
p    | This is what the UI looked like when I first joined the project.
p    | #Christmas tree #Unclear IA #Long scroll #Content gets lost
h3   | Explored Modal-Based Details (users’ fav)
p    | Rebalanced the information hierarchy across multiple layers of detail
p    | Consolidated all detailed information in a modal to minimise visual noise
p    | (blocked by dev team due to tech constrains)
h3   | Hybrid Tabs + Accordion (final)
p    | Parent accordions organised by document section (mirroring contract order)
p    | Enable users to do side-by-side comparison with one click
h2   | Design Decision
p    | Detected wording is compared against preferred language in a clear side-by-side view with concise risk summaries, enabling decisions in seconds.
h3   | AI-Powered Comparison with Source Verification
h3   | Human-in-the-Loop Machine Training
p    | Underwriters can quickly verify or relink AI-detected text within the “Text” view, embedding feedback directly into their normal review workflow. Each correction improves model accuracy over time, ensuring the system evolves with real user judgement.
h2   | Natural Review Workflow
p    | Key validation actions are surfaced at the point of review, enabling one-step decisions and preserving underwriters’ analytical flow.
h2   | Action List for Faster Collaboration
p    | Instead of jumping between components to recall what needs discussion, underwriters get a consolidated view of every flagged item, suggested alternative, and comment at a glance. When everything checks out, a single 'Finish Validation' click closes the loop.
